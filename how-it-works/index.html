numbers mean.

Your plan fixes the area, platforms, placements, time window and objective. Reporting keeps observations, estimates and commercial outcomes distinct.

Delivery and frequency

Impressions count ad deliveries. Reported reach describes the platform’s estimated unique accounts or people reached in the stated reporting scope. Frequency is impressions divided by reach in that same scope.

Daily reach should not be summed into a weekly unique count. Separate campaign reach should not be added into a deduplicated audience. An average frequency does not tell you how many ads every individual saw.

For a network of advertisers, the report must identify how overlapping audiences and any population estimate are handled. Carlton Landing’s network exposure estimate is labeled separately from a single campaign’s reported frequency.

Goal progress and saturation

A frequency goal is a target agreed in your plan. In the illustrative week, Wednesday’s 6.0 frequency divided by the 8.0 goal gives 75% of the daily frequency goal. That is not 75% of all advertising in the area.

A claim about share of advertising inventory needs both our delivered impressions and a supported total-inventory denominator for the same area, surfaces and period. When that denominator is unavailable, we report delivery and goal progress without presenting them as auction share.

Configured cap, paid cost and budget

A bid cap is a configured bidding control. Its units depend on the campaign’s optimization. The walkthrough explicitly assumes impression-optimized bidding and a $14 CPM cap. That setting is different from the paid CPM calculated from delivered impressions.

Paid CPM = actual spend ÷ impressions × 1,000. A daily budget is another setting. Actual spend can vary while the configured budget remains unchanged. In the example, spend goes from $198 to $240 within the unchanged $300 daily setting.

Customer price also includes service work. The sample scope separates its media allocation from creative, operation and reporting. Its price range is a calculated planning example, not a delivery forecast.

What competitor evidence can tell you

A reported ad sighting can document an advertiser, creative, place and time of observation. Our own cost and delivery changes can flag conditions worth investigating. Neither reveals another advertiser’s private audience settings or proves they intended to target a specific person or company.

Creative changes, placement mix, timing and our own bid or budget changes can also affect delivery. They belong in the explanation.

RevFirma recommends. You authorize. The platform serves.

The plan defines permissions before launch. Recommendations, authorization, recorded execution and later observations are distinct. RevFirma provides the relevant decision evidence during your review; operator approval tools are not a public client portal. A record of advice alone does not mean a change was applied.

The public walkthrough is fictional and cannot change advertising. In that scenario, the customer authorizes replacement creative while keeping the bid cap and budget setting fixed.

Reporting and business outcomes

Delivery, clicks, completed purchases and incremental sales answer different questions. Conversion tracking can connect observed actions to advertising under a stated attribution rule. Measuring incremental lift requires a design that can evaluate what would have happened without the campaign.

Historical case studies describe the results reported in those documents. They do not establish a guaranteed outcome for another business. Read the original Nabors case study.
